[[155]] Matching on class composition does not change this picture. When blocks are compared only within strata that share the block class set and the history class set, and further within a 0.1 prior bin, the matched difference is -0.104 with none of the 32 subjects positive (six specifications, 734-930 strata, all within 0.004 of each other). What this shows is robustness of the negative effect to how the strata are defined: it does not show that class composition is not the mechanism, because the composition of a block and its history is exactly the quantity being held fixed, and the oracle decomposition of Section 4.6 already points at the mix component.

[[156]] An independent negative control points the same way. On a P300 speller dataset that took no part in development (16 subjects, single session, target prior 0.14), the source is the first half of each subject's epochs and the target the second half, so the two share their class composition and no drift separates them. The pre-registered prediction was that the correction would hurt; it did not. The measured effect is -0.0005 with a 95 per cent interval of [-0.0024, +0.0012] and 7 of 16 subjects positive. The interval lies inside an equivalence margin of 0.02 on balanced accuracy, but the pre-registered run decoded at chance and the exploratory run only reached 0.50-0.78, so we report that no reliable gain or loss was observed under this protocol and we do not claim that the effect is zero. The diagnosis we do offer is narrower: with source and target sharing their class composition, the displacement the correction removes is approximately zero, which is the branch of the theory that predicts no change. The prediction was wrong about the sign, the diagnosis explains why, and both are reported.

[[157]] A second independent corpus has the cross-session structure this paper is about and gives a third answer. The BCI-challenge error-potential data (16 subjects, five sessions each, four consecutive forward pairs) give an overall mean change of -0.0044 with a 95 per cent interval of [-0.0166, +0.0075] and 6 of 16 subjects positive. Conditioning on decoding quality changes the picture: in the six subjects with balanced accuracy at least 0.52 the mean change is -0.0239 [-0.0415, -0.0093], and the per-subject correlation between balanced accuracy and the correction effect is -0.71 (p < 0.01). That subset was chosen after seeing the data and is exploratory, not a replication; what it does show is that the near-zero average is not evidence that the correction is harmless where the decoder works.
